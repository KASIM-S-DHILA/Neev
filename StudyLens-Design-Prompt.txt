STUDYLENS: STUDENT DESKTOP APP, PRODUCT DESIGN BRIEF

TASK
Redesign StudyLens as a student-only Electron desktop application. This is a product design task. Produce separate, cohesive, editable desktop mockups. Do not write application code.

PRIMARY GOAL
Reduce visual cognitive load. A student should immediately understand:
1. Where they are.
2. What they are studying.
3. What to do next.

Principles: progressive disclosure, restrained visual hierarchy, one main task per screen.
Avoid: dashboard clutter, excessive badges, large statistics panels, gamified streaks, decorative illustrations.

=====================================================
GLOBAL RULES (apply to every screen)
=====================================================

WINDOW
- Design for 1366 x 768 laptop screens. The layout must also be comfortable at smaller window sizes.
- Use internal scrolling within the desktop window.
- When space is limited, collapse the sidebar and show source evidence as a closable overlay rather than squeezing the answer column.
- Avoid horizontal scrolling for ordinary study content.

SHELL AND NAVIGATION
- Compact Windows titlebar with Chrome-style study tabs.
  - Each tab preserves its subject, topic, activity, and conversation.
  - The "+" button opens a quiet Workspace start screen.
  - Opening a tab must not automatically create a subject or start a conversation.
  - Show clear active-tab and close-tab states.
- Information hierarchy: Workspace > Subjects > Topics > Learn / Ask / Practice.
  Example: Semester 3 > Probability > Conditional probability > Ask
- Collapsible contextual sidebar containing, in order:
  1. Workspace switcher
  2. Workspace and Today
  3. Subjects
  4. Topics within the active subject
  5. Subject materials
  6. Settings (at the bottom)
- Learn / Ask / Practice live inside the topic view, not in the sidebar.
- Course map, Revision, and Schedule are reached through relevant subject/topic views. Do not place every feature permanently in the sidebar.

DESIGN CHARACTER: A PERSONAL STUDY DESK
- The interface should feel like a thoughtfully organized study notebook: warm, editorial, focused, and inviting.
- Let the student's actual material provide visual interest: document excerpts, highlighted passages, equations, lecture frames, bookmarks, and small prerequisite diagrams.
- Use different compositions for different tasks:
  - Workspace: an editorial overview.
  - Materials: an organized library.
  - Tutor: a readable explanation with contextual evidence.
  - Assessment: a focused question sheet.
  - Review: annotated answers.
  - Schedule: a practical agenda.
- Avoid repeating the same rounded-card layout across every screen.
- Do not use sparkles, AI orbs, robot avatars, decorative charts, or generic motivational slogans.
- Engagement comes from meaningful interaction: selecting evidence, revealing a flashcard, bookmarking an explanation, and understanding why a topic is recommended.
- Use restrained hover, selection, and transition states. Motion must serve orientation or feedback and respect reduced-motion preferences.

VISUAL SYSTEM
- Canvas: warm off-white #FBFBFA
- Sidebar and tab strip: #F7F6F3
- Cards and reading surfaces: white
- Borders: #E8E6E1
- Primary action: muted blue #2E5B88
- Main text: #1E232A
- Secondary text: #6E7480 (maintain accessible contrast)
- Source citations: teal #0D9488
- Status: restrained amber/green, always accompanied by text
- Typography: Inter for controls and body text. Use Source Serif 4 selectively for topic titles and reading headings. Maintain readable mathematics and appropriate Hindi font support. Monospace only for timestamps, source locators, and code.
- Readable body text, generous line spacing, consistent spacing, modest corner radii, subtle borders.
- Avoid: gradients, glass effects, excessive shadows, oversized headings.

CONSISTENCY AND STATES
- Use the same titlebar, tabs, sidebar, typography, and spacing on every screen.
- Design loading, empty, partial, error, and offline states wherever relevant.
- Long-running work shows nonblocking progress.
- Keep answer text readable when the source panel opens.
- Use text and icons alongside color.
- Avoid unnecessary panels and repeated composers.

SAMPLE CONTENT
- Use consistent Probability-course sample content throughout.
- Use clearly fictional demo content for quiz scores, file progress, and student history. Never present invented benchmark results, accuracy metrics, or effectiveness claims as measured facts.
- Wherever the screening example (Bayes' theorem) appears, use the same figures everywhere: 1% prevalence, 99% sensitivity, 5% false-positive rate. Per 10,000 people: 99 true positives, 495 false positives, 594 positive tests, P(disease | positive) = 16.7%, 83.3% of positive results are false positives.

OUT OF SCOPE
- Live audio tutoring and microphone-based conversation are ON HOLD. Do not include live voice-call controls.
- Teacher or admin views: none. Student-only.

=====================================================
SCREENS AND STATES
=====================================================

1. WORKSPACE / NEW TAB
Quiet landing view containing:
- One recommended next action, with an expandable "Why recommended?"
- Recent subjects
- A small Today agenda
- Create subject and Add materials as secondary actions
Do not place a tutor composer on this screen.

2. SUBJECT / TOPICS
- Topics as clean, scannable rows.
- Evidence-based statuses: Not assessed / Needs practice / Doing well.
- "Review due" is a separate memory reminder, not a mastery category.
- Avoid precise mastery percentages when evidence is insufficient.
- Opening a topic reveals Learn / Ask / Practice and its relevant sources.

3. NEW STUDENT STARTING POINT
- Brief intake, one step at a time: study goal, familiar topics, optional exam date.
- Offer a diagnostic quiz once course materials are ready.
- The student may begin studying before completing the diagnostic.
- Label the initial learner baseline as provisional.

4. MATERIALS / MULTIMODAL UPLOAD
- Supported types: textbooks/PDFs, slides, video, audio, images.
- Per-file states (compact): Queued > Processing > Ready. Also Partial, Failed, Cancelled.
- Show meaningful processing stages, plus Retry, Cancel, and Review actions.
- Partial extraction must clearly identify which sections are unavailable.
- Ready materials stay usable while other files process.
- Preserve filename, version, and original source location.
- Compact upload area plus a readable file list.
- Never block the interface with a full-screen processing modal.

5. GROUNDED TUTOR
- Show a short, source-backed answer first.
- "Explain step by step" and "Go deeper" are secondary actions.
- Exact citation chips, for example:
  - Textbook . p. 42
  - Slides . slide 8
  - Lecture 3 . 12:40
- The source viewer opens on the RIGHT only when a citation is selected. It has a close action and highlights the cited passage or relevant frame.
- One bottom conversation composer.
- Clearly distinguish these five evidence states:
  - Supported by uploaded materials
  - Partially supported
  - Evidence not found
  - Material still processing
  - Outside knowledge
- Explanation language options: English / Hindi / Hinglish. Original source evidence stays unchanged.

6. WEB SEARCH PERMISSION AND RESULTS
Web search uses SearXNG.
- After insufficient evidence, offer explicit permission for this question only:
  - Show the proposed search query.
  - Buttons: "Search the web" and "Keep using my materials".
- Do not preselect consent. Do not search automatically.
- Permission applies only to the current question or assessment.
- Design separate BEFORE-search and AFTER-search states.
- After search: label external information clearly and cite its source.
- Offer "Add this source to [subject]" as a deliberate action. Never silently add web material to the knowledge base.
- Generated explanations and saved notes must not appear as original course sources.

7. ASSESSMENT SETUP
Selectable options:
- Quick Quiz or Mock Exam
- Subject and topics
- Course materials
- Question types: MCQ, short answer, numerical
- Difficulty
- Question count
- Optional time limit
- Optional Web Challenge, with explicit permission for this assessment only.
Course-material questions and external challenge questions must be distinguishable.
Include preparation/validation progress and an actionable failure state.

8. ASSESSMENT QUESTION
- One question at a time, with restrained progress.
- Show representative MCQ, short-answer, and numerical layouts.
- Numerical questions support units and optional working.
- Every answer requires an initially unselected confidence choice: Guessing / Unsure / Confident. Confidence does not change exam marks.
- Quick Quiz: feedback can appear after checking an answer.
- Mock Exam: feedback only after final submission.
- Do not reveal answer keys or source passages before answering.
- Provide Skip or Mark for review where appropriate.
- Show a clear "Pending review" state when grading is uncertain.

9. ASSESSMENT REPORT
Priorities, in order:
1. Weak topics
2. Possible misconceptions, worded as hypotheses
3. Recommended next action
- Show score and evidence counts quietly.
- Keep Web Challenge performance separate from course mastery.
- Avoid large charts that add little value.

10. QUESTION REVIEW / ASK DOUBT
- Each question begins as a compact summary row.
- Expanding it reveals the student answer, verified answer, worked explanation, and original-source citations.
- "Ask doubt" and "Report problem" remain available for every question.
- "Ask doubt" opens a conversation attached to that specific question: show the question context clearly and use one doubt composer.
- Only one question's doubt conversation is open at a time.
- Support rechecking of disputed questions and corrected-grading states.

11. COURSE FLOW MAP
- Default: a small local map showing the current topic, its immediate prerequisites, and the suggested next topic.
- "Show full map" is secondary.
- Inferred connections are marked "Suggested".
- Nodes open topic details and study actions.
- Prerequisites guide students but never prevent them from studying later topics.

12. TARGETED REVISION
- Generate revision material for chosen or weak topics: flashcards, revision slides, audio briefs.
- One active format at a time.
- Show citations and a "Generated study aid" label.
- Flashcards use deliberate Reveal and recall-rating actions.
- Reading revision material alone must not imply improved mastery.
- No live voice controls (see Out of Scope).

13. STUDY SCHEDULE / TODAY
- Show Today first, with an optional full-calendar view.
- Build a realistic plan from: exam date and scope, available study time, weak topics, prerequisites, review timing, mixed practice.
- Actions: Edit availability, Defer, Reschedule.
- "Why scheduled?" explanation available on demand.
- Missing a planned session must not automatically reduce mastery.
- Reminder notifications require explicit opt-in.

14. SETTINGS / OPTIONAL INSPECT
- Keep technical details off ordinary student screens.
- Model/provider settings, processing diagnostics, and evaluation tools live inside Settings.
- Do not expose FAISS, token budgets, learner algorithms, or RAM charts on the dashboard. Do not invent benchmark results.
- The learner model includes: knowledge tracing, ability/difficulty estimation, practice history, prerequisites, misconception evidence, confidence calibration, spaced repetition, active recall, and interleaving. Translate these into simple student recommendations, with explanations available on demand.

=====================================================
DELIVERABLES
=====================================================
- First establish the design language using three separate screens:
  1. Workspace / New Tab.
  2. Grounded Tutor.
  3. Assessment Question (MCQ).
- Present these three screens for design-direction approval before generating the remaining screens.
- Once that direction is approved, extend the same system to the remaining screens.
- Add supporting states to the relevant screens in later batches: loading, empty, partial, error, offline, before/after web search, pending review, corrected grading, and the short-answer/numerical assessment layouts.
- Keep screen numbers and mockup labels outside the app interface.
- Clearly identify the main happy-path mockups and supporting states.
- Show the actual desktop UI directly, without decorative device mockups.
